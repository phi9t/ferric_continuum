* Summary

This effort uses the evidence-bound agentic engineering method to define the
remaining DeepSeek V4.1 work after the current CPU/tiny-fixture implementation.
The already-landed work covers the requested architecture concepts at source and
tiny-runtime level: CED, Engram, CSA2, the DeepSeek V4 CSA contrast, the
V3.2-origin indexer lineage with current V4.1 behavior, sliding-window
attention, and DSpark MTP.

Execution contract:

- Contract: =contract.toml=

The remaining work is not to re-open those as vague implementation tasks. The
remaining work is to produce stronger evidence for release use:

- real released-weight parity for text, multimodal, and DSpark paths;
- native quantized FP8/FP4 execution instead of eager f32 dequantization;
- tensor-parallel serving parity beyond static shard-layout checks;
- long-context cache/indexer stress evidence for SWA, CSA2, and CED;
- an explicit DSpark acceptance-loop decision based on upstream behavior;
- the lower-priority architecture-deepening follow-ups left by ticket 29;
- a final release-candidate evidence packet that separates completion from
  publication.

* Outcome Packet

Outcome:

- DeepSeek V4.1 in =tnsr= has a release-candidate evidence package that states
  exactly which claims are proven against local source, tiny fixtures, GPU
  execution, real released weights, tensor-parallel serving, and long-context
  behavior.

Why this matters:

- The current implementation is inspectable and source-grounded, but production
  use needs candidate-bound evidence on the same surfaces users will exercise:
  real checkpoints, device execution, cache growth, quantized kernels, and
  serving topology.

Mode:

- The whole effort is a gated delivery program made of delivery, scout, and
  experiment tickets. A scout may close with "unknown" when it produces bounded
  evidence and follow-up tickets.

Non-goals:

- No public release, push, merge, or external publication without explicit
  human authorization.
- No claim that tiny-fixture parity proves real released-weight parity.
- No claim that the CPU f32 dequantized path proves native FP8/FP4 performance.
- No speculative decoding acceptance loop unless ticket 05 explicitly finds an
  upstream-faithful contract or the human approves a tnsr-native experiment.
